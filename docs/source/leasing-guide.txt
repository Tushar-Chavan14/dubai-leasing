Part 1: Plain-English guide
What this document is
We will use AI helpers to run most of the Dubai leasing process, with people approving every step that has legal or money impact. This part explains, in plain English, how leasing works in Dubai and how our AI design fits into it.
It is written for anyone on the team, including people with no real estate or technical background. Abu Dhabi follows a similar process with different rules; it will get its own version later.
Words you will see
Word
What it means in simple terms
Landlord
The owner of the property. We usually act for the landlord as the developer or property manager.
Tenant
The person or company renting the property.
Lease / tenancy contract
The rental agreement between landlord and tenant. In Dubai it uses a standard government format.
DLD
Dubai Land Department, the government body for property in Dubai.
RERA
The part of DLD that regulates renting.
Ejari
Dubai's official system for registering every lease. A lease that is not registered in Ejari is not recognised by the government.
Smart Rental Index
DLD's official tool that shows the normal rent for similar homes. It decides how much rent can go up at renewal.
DEWA
Dubai's electricity and water company. The tenant cannot get power and water without a registered lease.
Notice
A formal written message to the tenant, for example "your rent will increase" or "please vacate". The law sets how early it must be sent.
PDC
Post-dated cheques. Tenants in Dubai often pay rent with several cheques dated through the year.
Security deposit
Money the tenant pays at the start, returned at the end minus any damage.
RDC
Rental Disputes Centre, where landlord-tenant disagreements go.
Agent (AI agent)
A piece of AI software that does one job, such as reading an ID card or drafting a contract.

The leasing journey, step by step
Every lease goes through the same 11 steps, from an empty unit to the tenant moving out. Think of it like a hotel stay that lasts a year: get the room ready, take the booking, check in, look after the guest, and check out.
Get the unit ready. Make sure the property is registered with DLD and approved for rent. Decide the asking rent.
Find a tenant. Answer enquiries from interested people and book viewings.
Check the applicant. Collect their Emirates ID or passport and visa (or trade license for a company) and check the details are correct.
Agree the offer. Agree the rent, number of cheques, deposit and move-in date. Take a reservation payment.
Prepare and sign the contract. Fill in the standard Dubai contract, plus an addendum for any special terms. Both sides sign.
Register the lease in Ejari. This makes the lease official. Without it, the tenant cannot connect electricity and water.
Move the tenant in. Activate DEWA, hand over keys and access cards, and record the condition of the unit with photos.
Look after the tenancy. Collect rent, follow up late payments and bounced cheques, and handle complaints and maintenance.
Renew the lease. Before the lease ends, decide whether to renew and whether rent can go up. Send any notice on time.
Move the tenant out. Inspect the unit, compare it with move-in photos, return the deposit minus any damage, and cancel the Ejari registration.
Handle disputes, if any. If the landlord and tenant disagree, prepare the case for the Rental Disputes Centre.
After step 10, the unit goes back to step 1 for the next tenant.

How we make it agentic
"Agentic" means AI helpers do the work of each step on their own, instead of a person doing it by hand. Our design has four parts that work like a well-run office.
The Manager keeps a file for every lease. It knows which step each lease is on, what comes next and every deadline. It wakes up the right helper at the right time, even if that is 12 months away.
The Rulebook holds Dubai's laws and our company limits: notice periods, rent caps, allowed discounts. It gives the same answer every time.
The AI helpers each do one job well, such as reading an ID card or drafting a contract. They never decide legal values themselves; they ask the Rulebook.
People approve every step that creates a legal or money commitment. The Manager pauses and waits for their approval.

The Manager is in charge, not an AI. Leasing has fixed legal deadlines, so the order of steps must be predictable and every decision must be traceable if a dispute reaches the Rental Disputes Centre.

Every helper gets the same kind of input and must give its answer in a fixed format. This is what allows each team member to build their helper separately without breaking the others.
Three examples
Example 1: a new tenant
Aisha messages on WhatsApp asking about a 1-bedroom flat.
The Leasing Assistant answers her questions and books a viewing for Saturday.
After the viewing, she uploads her Emirates ID. The KYC helper reads it and confirms the details.
The Offer helper proposes 4 cheques and a 5% deposit, within company limits. Aisha accepts and pays the reservation.
The Contract helper fills the standard contract using her verified details. A leasing officer reviews and approves it. Aisha signs.
The Registration helper prepares the Ejari application. The officer submits it and Ejari is issued.
The Manager marks the lease as active and sets reminders for every future date: rent due dates, the renewal window and the lease end.
Example 2: renewing a lease
The lease ends on 31 December. The Manager works backwards from that date:
13 months before: asks the landlord whether they plan to sell or use the property themselves. If yes, a person handles the 12-month legal notice.
5 months before: the Renewal helper checks the Smart Rental Index and works out the maximum legal increase.
4 months before: the draft notice goes to a manager to approve.
100 days before: the notice is sent and proof of delivery is saved.
90 days before: this is the legal deadline. If nobody approved in time, the system offers renewal at the same rent, so the lease is never left in limbo.
We send at 100 days, not 90, to leave 10 days of safety margin.
Example 3: when something goes wrong
Ejari rejects the registration. The Registration helper reads the reason, compares the details against the verified ID and fixes simple typing errors once. If it fails again, a person takes over. It never changes a contract term on its own.
A cheque bounces. Collections sends friendly reminders. The formal reminder needs a person's approval because it starts the legal 30-day clock.
A helper gives a messy answer. The system rejects it, asks the helper to try again, and if it still fails, sends the task to a person. Nothing moves forward on a bad answer.
What people decide, what we build first, and how we measure it
People always decide these
The AI prepares; a person approves. These actions create a legal or money commitment that can't be undone by trying again:
Approving the final contract
Submitting to Ejari (until we connect directly to the Ejari system)
Sending formal late-payment reminders
Sending any rent increase or "please vacate" notice
Refunds above a set amount
Any rent outside what the rulebook allows
Build order
The Manager, the Rulebook and the lease record. Everything else depends on these, so a technical person builds them first.
Renewal & Notice helper. It brings the most measurable value: missing the 90-day notice loses a full year of rent increase.
KYC, Contract and Registration helpers. These stop Ejari rejections caused by mismatched details.
Connect the three helpers already being built (Payment Reminder, Complaint Triage, Refund).
Leasing Assistant and Pricing. Lowest risk; many existing tools already do part of this.
Today, people will upload data using sheets. Later, we connect directly to DLD's Ejari system, which allows issuing, renewing and cancelling leases from our software. That connection requires working through a registered property management company (DLD API requirements).
